# Nano-Jev v0.1 vs baselines (2026-09-25)

Same test split for every model (held-out halves of HotpotQA / SQuAD 2.0 / MultiNLI
validation sets; see `v0.1.md`). Every model gets per-decision temperature scaling fitted
on the same calib split. Accuracy is after calibration; ECE shown raw → calibrated.
Speed = batched throughput on an RTX 3060 (ms per decision), not single-request latency.

| Model | Params | How it decides |
|---|---|---|
| **Nano-Jev v0.1** | 22M | MiniLM cross-encoder fine-tuned on these decision types (~15 min) |
| bge-reranker-v2-m3 | 568M | Off-the-shelf reranker score → logistic regression fitted on calib |
| Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 | 4B | Zero-shot prompt with option definitions; answer-letter probabilities from one forward pass |
| MiniLM init (untrained) | 22M | Nano-Jev's starting point, no fine-tuning |

## Accuracy (macro-F1)

| Decision | Nano-Jev 22M | bge-reranker 568M | Qwen3-4B | untrained init |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| relevance (3-way) | **0.798** (0.764) | 0.694 (0.618) | 0.660 (0.602) | 0.273 |
| sufficient | **0.759** (0.758) | 0.692 (0.692) | 0.706 (0.701) | 0.503 |
| grounded | 0.804 (0.804) | 0.798 (0.798) | **0.844** (0.844) | 0.540 |

## Calibration — ECE raw → calibrated (fitted temperature)

| Decision | Nano-Jev | bge-reranker* | Qwen3-4B |
|---|---|---|---|
| relevance | 0.061 → **0.020** (T 1.45) | 0.026 | 0.313 → 0.078 (T 13.5) |
| sufficient | 0.065 → **0.019** (T 1.38) | 0.022 | 0.282 → 0.045 (T 17.4) |
| grounded | 0.083 → 0.045 (T 1.23) | 0.028 | 0.155 → **0.019** (T 12.5) |

\*The reranker's probabilities come from a logistic regression fitted on calib, so they
are calibrated by construction (T = 1.00).

## Speed (ms per decision, batched, RTX 3060)

| Decision | Nano-Jev | bge-reranker | Qwen3-4B | Nano-Jev speed-up vs Qwen |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| relevance | **1.6** | 7.4 | 240.8 | ~150× |
| sufficient | **2.3** | 15.4 | 379.1 | ~165× |
| grounded | **0.6** | 3.5 | 54.5 | ~90× |

## Findings

1. **Nano-Jev wins relevance and sufficiency.** It beats a 25× larger reranker by 7–10
   points and a 180× larger prompted LLM by 5–14 points, at 90–165× the LLM's speed.
2. **The prompted LLM wins groundedness** (0.844 vs 0.804). Claim verification (NLI)
   needs more reasoning than a 22M model has. Candidate fix: a **cascade** that accepts
   confident Nano-Jev `grounded` verdicts and escalates the rest to Qwen3-4B (the
   JEV-as-a-Judge pattern).
3. **Raw LLM probabilities are badly overconfident** (ECE 0.16–0.31, temperatures 12–17).
   They are usable for thresholds only after calibration. Nano-Jev's raw probabilities are
   already close (T 1.2–1.5).
4. **Fine-tuning does the work.** The untrained init is at chance on everything.

## Caveats (read before citing)

- **In-distribution advantage.** Nano-Jev was trained on the train splits of the same
  three datasets; the baselines were not. This shows that task-specific training beats
  general models *on these tasks*. It does not yet show generalisation. **Next:** a
  held-out dataset (e.g. MuSiQue, or a domain corpus).
- **Label definitions.** Relevance labels come from HotpotQA rules ("partially relevant"
  = supporting paragraph without the answer string). The LLM sees plain-English
  definitions that may not match those rules exactly, which hurts it most on
  relevance.
- **One prompt, zero-shot, one LLM size.** No few-shot examples or prompt tuning, and no 8B.
- **Speed** is batched throughput on one GPU. Single-request latency will be higher for
  every model.
