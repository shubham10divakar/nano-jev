Dear Editor and Reviewers,

We thank the three reviewers for careful and constructive reports. The comments clustered around
five issues: the strength of the baselines, the K-pass design, the coverage of the cascade, the
calibration protocol and statistical robustness, plus RAG realism. Each needed new experiments,
and we ran them. In summary:

1. We **re-ran every experiment under a clean protocol**: checkpoint selection now uses a
   development split carved from the training pool. The calibration split is used only for
   temperatures, cascade thresholds and baseline mappings.
2. We **trained 5 seeds** of our model and of a new **fine-tuned 1-pass baseline** on the same
   backbone and data. We now report mean ± std, bootstrap confidence intervals and exact McNemar
   tests.
3. We **added compact groundedness checkers** (DeBERTa-v3 NLI, AlignScore, MiniCheck) and a
   multi-sentence RAG-style benchmark (**LLM-AggreFact**).
4. We **ran the cascade on all three decisions**, with held-out escalation rates.
5. We **added a truncation analysis**, a **systematic custom-option evaluation**, and a small
   **end-to-end RAG evaluation**.
6. We fixed all table inconsistencies. **Every table and every number in the text is now
   generated by one script** from the logged result files.

Point-by-point replies follow. Reviewer text is paraphrased in italics.

same 47,272 training examples, the same epochs, learning rate, batch size and development-set
selection, and 5 seeds. The input is `[CLS] question-template [SEP] context [SEP]`, with a shared
encoder and one head per decision type. This is both the requested fair fine-tuned baseline and
the K-pass vs 1-pass ablation.
[RESULT: K-pass vs 1-pass test accuracy per decision with ± std; McNemar p; ECE; latency.]
[If the two are within noise:] The 1-pass model matches K-pass accuracy at lower latency. We
therefore no longer present accuracy as an advantage of the K-pass design. Its remaining benefits

**R1.2 (custom options) — *The claim that arbitrary option sets work without retraining rests on
one anecdote.***
We replaced the anecdote with a systematic zero-shot evaluation on AG News (K=4), Yahoo Answers
(K=10) and Emotion (K=6) (Section 5.11, Table 16). The result is negative and we report it as
such. v0.1 is above chance but weak (0.396 / 0.239 / 0.334 vs. chance 0.250 / 0.100 / 0.167).
v1.0 is *below* chance on all three (0.186 / 0.056 / 0.138). We removed the claim from the
abstract, Section 3.2 and the contributions. The paper now states that custom options are an
interface property, not a demonstrated capability, and lists option-vocabulary training as future
work. [RESULT: revised-model column and the supervised-probe reference.]

## Reviewer 3

**R3.1 — *Baselines are weak or not task-specific.***
In addition to the 1-pass baseline (R1.1), Table 6 compares groundedness with DeBERTa-v3 NLI (base
and large, trained on MNLI/FEVER/ANLI), AlignScore-base and MiniCheck (RoBERTa-L, DeBERTa-L),
on MultiNLI, VitaminC and LLM-AggreFact. [RESULT] We also removed the phrase "to ensure fair
comparison". BGE-reranker and the prompted Qwen3-4B are now explicitly called zero-shot or adapted
*references* (Section 3.6).

**R3.2 — *The calibration split is also used for checkpoint selection.***
The reviewer is right; in the submitted version the epoch was selected on a slice of the
calibration split. We created a development split of 2,488 examples from the training pool (5%,
grouped by question so that no question appears in both), retrained every model, and refitted all
temperatures, thresholds and mappings on the untouched calibration split (Section 4.3). The only
result still from the old protocol is the base-model comparison (Table 3), which only motivates
the choice of encoder and is labelled as such.

**R3.3 — *Custom option sets.*** See R1.2.

**R3.4 — *Cascade coverage.*** See R2.2.

**R3.5 (and R2.3) — *Statistical robustness.***
CaliDec and the 1-pass baseline are trained with 5 seeds and reported as mean ± std. We add
bootstrap 95% confidence intervals (10,000 resamples) and exact McNemar tests with paired bootstrap
intervals for every close comparison (Section 4.4). For groundedness (n = 500) the 95% interval of
a single accuracy is about ±3 points, so we only call a difference significant where the paired
test supports it. [RESULT: CaliDec vs Qwen on MultiNLI, p = …; cascade vs each model, p = …]
